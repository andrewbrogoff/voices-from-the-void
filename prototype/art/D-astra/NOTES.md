# Option D — review notes

## What worked

The upright open fork is recognisable at a 32-screen-pixel GUI-scale-2 slot. The copper reads warm without orange noise, and the pale amethyst now has enough area to survive reduction. Pale green-yellow brass with one cool reflection separates the alloy from copper and gold. The late bell gains its unease from a missing rim and a dark cavity rather than ornament or purple effects. The jar's transparent upper volume remains visible above the contained peach essentia.

## Actual quality loop

I opened and inspected the generated PNGs after each pass, including the 32 px slot row on grey, light and dark grounds; enlarged images were secondary. The three checkpoints are retained under `previews/revisions/` and can be regenerated by the default build.

1. **Initial — `00-initial.png`.** Fork silhouette was clear, but the jewel was undersized. The ingot's full-width cyan stripe made it look layered. The bell was simply a normal grey bell. Jar rims and a full-width lid made the container read as a box, and its striped liquid top looked solid.
2. **Revision 1 — `01-openings.png`.** Enlarged the fork's amethyst. Shortened the cool stripe and darkened the ingot scores. Opened a deep asymmetric bell recess, removed a section of its right shell and lower rim. Narrowed the jar's neck/lid, reduced glass-edge alpha, and separated the meniscus from its top texture. Slot checks confirmed a more useful jar silhouette and a recognisably damaged bell; the lid marks still resembled a button.
3. **Revision 2 — `02-final.png`.** Tightened the jewel facets and added a short physical glint to the right copper tine. Gave the ingot a firmer lower edge. Drained the bell's teal glint and reinforced its surviving lower rim. Replaced the jar's parallel lid marks and blue square with stepped concentric seal rings; reduced shoulder opacity and bounded the vertical glint. Opened the final slot board and jar render: each remains identifiable across the three grounds. The jar's seal pattern is useful in the world render but not legible in its tiny slot.

## Sound check

Opened `previews/spectrogram.png` and read `previews/sound-stat.txt` and `previews/audio-metrics.json`. The spectrogram shows low paired tines, short higher modes below 2.5 kHz, a smoothly receding tail and an empty upper band. The decoded Vorbis is mono / 44.1 kHz / 2.60 s, peak **−7.054 dBFS**, RMS **−22.751 dBFS**, mean/DC **0.000001563**, and fraction of spectral energy above 10 kHz **1.006×10⁻⁸**. The final 10 ms decode to zero; the last sample is zero. No clipping or abrupt end is present. The first decoded sample is 0.000153 (about −76 dBFS), a negligible codec boundary residue rather than a full-level attack. I could not listen; the review page provides playback for human judgement.

## What fought me / weakest asset

**The jar is weakest.** Its five-element square body is economical, but still boxy; at slot size its fine seal and subtle reflected colours disappear. The native-size proof is visibly softer than the item sprites because it reduces a translucent Blender render. I kept the broader neck-and-lid silhouette instead of adding geometry to compensate. This is a proposed direction, not a claim that the jar's real-game inventory view has passed QA.

The brass also risks reading as a light ceramic or a greenish gold before a player learns the material. Broad value separation and the cool reflected band help, but that should be tested beside actual inventory materials in-game. The polished 32×32 look is intentionally more refined than vanilla and needs a coherent family of props to feel integrated.

The first complete rebuild comparison caught Blender's variable `Date` and `RenderTime` PNG fields. The rendered pixels were stable (the derived sheet and slot PNGs matched). A post-render metadata strip fixed file-level reproducibility while preserving decoded pixels and leaving the shared renderer untouched. Vorbis also needs a canonical stream serial and recomputed page CRCs; both normalisations live in this option.

## Provenance and limits

- Read the brief, lore, progression, context, licence notice, kept logo and icon. The working-tree icon is a violet VF monogram; the acoustic metal/glass cues came from the kept logo and the written lore. Neither branding asset was copied into this option.
- The requested research document is absent from the working tree. Reading the referenced research branch would require `git show`, contrary to this task's explicit no-git rule; it was not read.
- No prohibited reference images, mod archives, external sites, other option folders, image/audio models or GitHub were accessed. No vanilla texture was sampled, copied or traced. The supplied Blender helper opens the vanilla client archive as part of its existing startup, but this parentless model resolves every used texture locally.
- Only `D-astra/` was written. The shared tools were read and invoked unchanged; their temporary files were directed into this option's temporary directory. The default build removes that directory after use.
- `gen/build.sh` is the sole build entry point. Reproducibility results and compared hashes are in `previews/reproducibility.json`. Versions used: Python 3, Pillow 12.2.0, NumPy 2.4.4, SoX 14.4.2, Blender 5.2.2 LTS. Byte identity is checked on this installed toolchain, not promised across different encoder/render versions.
- No in-game client load, subjective audio listening, or long-session fatigue test was performed. Translucent rendering and inventory transforms need that final integration check.
